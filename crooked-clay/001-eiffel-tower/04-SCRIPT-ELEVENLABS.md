## BEAT 1 · La torre que iba a desaparecer · tomas 11-19
`170 palabras`

```
Paris, nineteen twenty-five. The Eiffel Tower was never supposed to last this long. It was built for the eighteen eighty-nine World's Fair, and its original permit was for just twenty years. Many Parisians had called it an eyesore from day one. By the nineteen-twenties, it needed constant painting and repairs, and they weren't cheap. One day, a newspaper ran a short piece about the cost of maintaining it. Most readers turned the page. One man read it twice. Victor Lustig was born in eighteen ninety in what is now the Czech Republic. He spoke several languages, dressed like an aristocrat, and had a scar along his left cheek. He learned his trade on luxury ocean liners, cheating wealthy passengers at cards. Reading that article, Lustig saw what nobody else did. If people believed the city might tear the tower down, someone could sell it as scrap metal. Over seven thousand tons of iron. All he needed was a buyer. And a story good enough to make that buyer believe it.
```

## BEAT 2 · La reunión secreta · tomas 20-28
`172 palabras`

```
First, he had official-looking government stationery made. He posed as a deputy director general of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs. His partner, a con man known as Dapper Dan Collins, played his assistant. Every detail was designed to look official. Then he wrote to six of the biggest scrap metal dealers in Paris. The invitation was to a confidential meeting at the Hôtel de Crillon, one of the most prestigious hotels in the city. Not a back alley. A palace. In a private room, Lustig gave them the news. The city could no longer afford the tower. It would be taken down and sold for scrap, and the government wanted it done quietly. If the public found out early, there would be outrage. That secrecy was the trap. It explained why the deal was unusual, why nobody should ask around, and why everything had to move fast. Then he drove the six men to inspect the tower in a rented limousine. Seeing it up close made the whole thing feel real.
```

## BEAT 3 · Escoger a la víctima · tomas 29-37

```
But Lustig wasn't really looking at the tower that day. He was studying the dealers. Who asked sharp questions. Who looked nervous. And who wanted this deal a little too much. He was choosing his victim. That man was André Poisson. He had money, but he felt like an outsider among the established businessmen of Paris. A deal this big, straight from the government, would finally put him in the inner circle. Lustig could see it in his eyes. Lustig asked the dealers to submit their bids. But the auction was theater. He had already picked the winner during the tour. The other five dealers never had a chance. Their bids didn't matter. Lustig only needed one name, and he already had it. There was just one problem. Poisson's wife didn't like it. Why all the secrecy? Why meet in a hotel instead of a government office? Why the rush? Poisson started to doubt. And a con man with a doubting victim has hours, not days.
```

## BEAT 4 · El soborno · tomas 38-46
`160 palabras`

```
So Lustig did something brilliant. He asked to see Poisson again, and this time he made a confession. Not that he was a con man. That he was corrupt. It's one of the smartest moves in the history of fraud. He explained that a government official's salary was modest, and a man in his position had expenses. In other words, he was asking for a bribe. He never had to say the word. And that was exactly what convinced Poisson. In Poisson's mind, only a real official would ask for a bribe. A fake one wouldn't dare. The request that should have exposed the scam became the proof that it was real. Poisson paid for the tower. And he paid the bribe. Lustig and Collins took a train to Vienna with a suitcase of cash and waited for the headlines. They never came. Reporting the scam meant Poisson admitting he'd been fooled, and that he'd tried to bribe an official.
```

## BEAT 5 · Otra vez · tomas 47-55
`146 palabras`

```
A normal criminal would have disappeared forever. Lustig did the opposite. Months later, he came back to Paris, to sell the Eiffel Tower again. He believed nobody had reported the first sale, and he was right. Same story. Same forged letters. A new group of scrap dealers. This time, the pitch was polished. He knew which questions the dealers would ask, and he had every answer ready. And it almost worked a second time. But this time, the chosen buyer went to the police before handing over the money. The story broke, and newspapers finally printed what Poisson had kept secret. Lustig and Collins vanished before the police could catch them. Lustig left Europe and sailed for the United States. In America, he'd find bigger targets, and he'd use dozens of fake names to reach them. One of them was the most dangerous man in Chicago.
```

## BEAT 6 · América y Al Capone · tomas 56-64
`173 palabras`

```
First, he sold the Rumanian Box. A small wooden box he claimed could copy hundred-dollar bills. He'd load it with real bills in advance, show it working, and sell it to greedy buyers for thousands of dollars. The box produced a few more bills over the next hours. By the time buyers realized it only made blank paper, Lustig was long gone. Many never reported it, for the same reason Poisson didn't: they had been trying to print money themselves. Then came his boldest move. He went to Al Capone, asked him for fifty thousand dollars, and promised to double it in two months. Capone handed it over. Lustig put the money in a safe deposit box, and did absolutely nothing with it. Two months later, he returned every dollar, apologizing that the deal had failed. Capone was so impressed by his honesty that he gave him five thousand dollars. That was the plan all along. Lustig never wanted the fifty thousand. He wanted Capone's trust, and the reward that came with it.
```

## BEAT 7 · Dinero falso y Alcatraz · tomas 65-73
`162 palabras`

```
Then Lustig made the mistake that ended his career. He moved into counterfeiting. With a skilled partner, he produced fake bills so convincing that they spread across the country. But fake money wasn't a private scam with one embarrassed victim. It was a federal crime. That put him against a new opponent: the U.S. Secret Service. In nineteen thirty-five, agents arrested him in New York. In his pocket was a key to a locker holding counterfeit money and printing plates. The day before his trial, he escaped. He made a rope from bedsheets and climbed down the outside of a federal detention building in Manhattan, pretending to clean the windows as people watched. He was free for less than a month. Agents found him in Pittsburgh. This time, there was no story to tell. He was sentenced to twenty years and sent to Alcatraz. The man who sold the Eiffel Tower ended up on an island he couldn't talk his way off.
```
